National Funded Research (PKM-RE 2023)

Biosorbent Development for Pb(II) Heavy Metal Reduction

Utilizing Red Jabon Leaf Waste via NaOH-Ultrasonic Activation

Directed and executed this nationally funded research project under the PKM-RE 2023 program, secured through the Ministry of Education and Culture (Belmawa). As the Team Leader, I spearheaded the project from its inception (drafting the successful grant proposal and managing administrative compliance) through to the rigorous laboratory execution. ​Taking a highly hands-on approach, I drove the primary laboratory operations, which included sourcing raw materials, setting up the equipment, and actively synthesizing the biosorbent. Furthermore, I facilitated team coordination to maintain research momentum and led the comprehensive quantitative data processing to evaluate our analytical findings.

Core Technical Execution

Biosorbent Preparation

Engineered an eco-friendly and cost-effective biosorbent using red jabon leaf waste (Anthocephalus macrophyllus). Executed mechanical preparation (sieving to 100-120 mesh) and chemical activation using NaOH concentrations of 0.1, 0.3, and 0.5 M to break down lignocellulosic bonds and enlarge pore structures.

Adsorption Testing

Evaluated the biosorbent's efficiency in reducing Pb(II) heavy metal ions. Conducted rigorous equilibrium time testing on artificial Pb(II) solutions (ranging from 50 to 500 mg/L) and validated the application using actual hospital wastewater from Klinik Pratama USK.

Instrumental Analysis

Characterized the physical morphology and functional groups of the biosorbent using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and Fourier Transform InfraRed Spectroscopy (FTIR). Analyzed the final adsorption capacity and kinetic models quantitatively using an Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometer (AAS).
