Preliminary Design of an Ethylene Glycol Plant

From Coal Gasification Route (Capacity: 50,000 Tons/Year)

Project Overview & Execution

Developed a comprehensive preliminary plant design framework for producing 50,000 tons/year of Ethylene Glycol via coal gasification, offering a sustainable alternative to conventional petroleum-based routes. The plant layout was strategically planned for Separi Village, Kutai Kartanegara, utilizing the Mahakam River for process water supply.

Taking full ownership of the technical design, I independently formulated rigorous mass and energy balances across all core processing units. This included managing a 4,587 kg/hour coal feedstock and designing the entire operational lifecycle—from syngas generation and purification to catalytic hydrogenation—achieving a final product purity of 99%.

Process Synthesis & Optimization

  • Engineered entrained-bed gasification loops operating at 1,500°C and 40 bar, integrated with a 15% MEA absorption tower (T-201) for critical acid gas removal.
  • Designed a multi-stage catalytic reactor network for methyl nitrite synthesis (R-301), dimethyl oxalate formation (R-302), and final hydrogenation (R-303).
  • Optimized product separation using rigorous distillation columns (T-301, T-401) to achieve 99% purity, strategically recycling methanol by-products to minimize waste.

Economic Feasibility Analysis

  • Evaluated the plant's financial viability by calculating total fixed and variable costs, alongside initial investment requirements derived from equipment sizing.
  • Modeled comprehensive cash flows, achieving a Pay-Out Time (POT) of 3.57 years and a Break-Even Point (BEP) of 25.71%.
  • Validated robust project profitability with an Internal Rate of Return (IRR) of 24.01%, comfortably surpassing the 18.00% MARR benchmark.
